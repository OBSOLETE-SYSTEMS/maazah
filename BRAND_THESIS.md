The wordmark, the arch, the twin-women-under-the-palm icon all serve that meaning. Most CPG brands have to *earn* the word "joy" in their copy. Maazah is literally named it.
2. **Two sisters, one mom's recipe — and the math of that is the moat.** Yasameen and Sheilla Sajady, first-generation Afghan Americans, Minneapolis. The brand started with Mom's magical green sauce, slung from a farmers market table. Every Maazah product traces back to that origin, and every founder photo confirms it — the sister duo is not a marketing construct, it's the operating reality. Competing better-for-you sauce brands either have one founder or a CPG-vet operator team. Two sisters working from their mother's kitchen is uncopiable.
3. **"Magic" is the master metaphor, and it's already wired across every surface.** The wordmark, the labels ("MAGIC SAUCE" / "MAGIC RECIPE" / "FAMILY RECIPE"), the hero line ("MAGIC IS REAL. ## The opportunity — three converging conditions

### 1. The Costco / Sprouts trial-to-repeat window is open NOW and finite

Maazah just hit Midwest Costco shelves (May 2026). The repeat-velocity window for a new Costco SKU is roughly 8-12 weeks before the buyer reviews unit-per-store-per-week math against the resets calendar. Sprouts nationwide rolled in December 2025 — those stores are in their second-half-of-year repeat data right now. The strategic question isn't "should we drive trial?" — Costco's foot traffic does that. The strategic question is: **what does the consumer see in their feed the week AFTER they buy the tub that gets them to use it before it expires?**

The engine's job in this window is to flood the feed with usage multiplication — "put it on everything" briefs, snackle-box builds, the lakeside hang, the lunchbox dollop, the air-fryer reveal, the "wait, on pizza?" surprise. That's how a CPG brand earns the second tub.

### 2. The "lentil dip" category doesn't exist in shopper heads yet — and that's the moat

Walk into Costco and try to find the "lentil dip" set. There isn't one. Maazah's tubs sit in the refrigerated dip/hummus section, where the shopper's mental model is *hummus* — Sabra, Cedar's, Boar's Head. The lentil dip is a NEW category trying to land inside an existing one. That's a hard sell unless the brand actively teaches the category at the moment of trial.

Most category-creator brands lose this fight because they either (a) try to be "hummus but better" (Maazah is wisely not doing this), or (b) lead with the ingredient story so dryly that no one cares about the difference. The opening is to lead with **what lentils give you that chickpeas don't** — protein density, fiber, fullness — wrapped in the joy-language Maazah already speaks. "Lentil dip — full-on. Hummus is just lonely." That kind of line.

The engine's job: build a recurring brief lane that translates the category truth (lentils ≠ chickpeas, dip ≠ spread, the protein math is different, the texture is different, the heritage is Middle Eastern but rendered Afghan-American) without ever sounding like a chemistry lesson.

### 3. Afghan-American storytelling is at peak cultural permission

The cultural conditions for Maazah's heritage story are stronger right now than at any point in the brand's history:

- **First-gen identity content is one of TikTok's biggest evergreen categories** — #firstgen, #immigrantparents, #immigrantkids, "things my mom said that hit different now" — these are pillars of the platform, not trends.
- **The post-2021 Afghan diaspora has built public voice.** Restaurants like Lapis (D.C.), Choopan (NYC, Choopan Halal), Bamiyan (Brooklyn), and writers like Humaira Ghilzai have lifted Afghan food's visibility. The category is more searchable + more newsworthy than it was 5 years ago.
- **Mom-of-X / "what my mom packed me" / heritage-recipe content** is having a sustained moment across food TikTok and IG Reels, driven by creators like Pa's recipes, Maangchi-style heritage-cooking accounts, and the *Cherry Bombe* generational-family-recipe issues.
- **The "first-gen founder" arc** is what's selling business-press features in 2026 — Yasameen + Sheilla's story is the exact arc that lands in Forbes' 30-Under-30, *Cherry Bombe* lists, *Goldhouse A100*, *Food & Wine* Game Changers.

The engine's job: keep the heritage lane *active* — not as occasional storytelling garnish, as a structural pillar with weekly briefs. The cultural moment doesn't last forever; the founders should be the ones speaking into it while it's open.

## The channel reality — what's working, what's broken

A calibrated read of @maazahmagic across IG and TikTok, with engagement data drawn from the public profile (2026-05-26).

**Instagram is doing the work.** Sister-led milestone posts are landing — the Costco-cart push (May 17): 426 likes. The Sprouts nationwide announcement (Dec 18): 353. Expo West Top 10 Moments (Mar 17): 486. Brand-collab and external-feature content tracks 143–198 likes. The 41-like lakeside snackle box (May 24) outperformed the 28-like falafel-bowl recipe Reel from four days earlier — and the snackle box took roughly 1/10th the production effort. The pattern is unambiguous: **sister-led + community-tagged content compounds; heavily-produced recipe Reels underperform their effort.**

**TikTok is severely under-leveraged.** The same Costco-cart push that landed 426 likes on IG: **11 likes on TikTok**. The Times Square billboard moment: 2 likes. The Specialty Food Show prep: 3. The Taylor Swift Fallon meme-reaction: 2. The Kendrick Super Bowl wings recipe: 4. **Cross-posted IG content underperforms by ~10-30× on TikTok.** The format register is wrong — IG's groove-audio + lifestyle-narrative pattern doesn't match what TikTok's discovery algorithm wants.

**Except one post.** *"What is chutney?"* (Nov 21, 2024) — period-spaced descriptors ("Herby. Vibrant. Fresh."), the killer line *"chutney was basically our ketchup,"* category-education delivered in brand voice over food b-roll. Engagement: **211 likes, 14 comments, 71 saves.** That's 30× their normal TikTok baseline, and the 71 saves are the kicker — saves are the buyer-readable signal of *"this brand is teaching the category."*

**The strategic implication:** TikTok isn't "another channel to post the same thing." It's a different lane that wants category-education, period-spaced format, meme-fluent audio, and text-overlay-led discovery content. The engine's playbook on TikTok is: *answer the questions shoppers don't know to ask* — what is chutney, what's the difference between lentil dip and hummus, why is Afghan food different from Middle Eastern, what makes a "magic" sauce magic — every week, in the voice that already works.

**The buyer-signal implication:** a wholesale buyer pulling up Maazah today sees a strong IG and a flat TikTok. That cross-channel incoherence is a soft negative on the renewal/extension review. Fixing the TikTok format register isn't a vanity exercise — it's a buyer-signal upgrade. **Top three strategic priorities, ranked:** (1) Costco repeat-window content (the next 8-12 weeks); (2) TikTok format-register fix (recurring category-explainer weekly); (3) UGC amplification + Wholesale Lens visibility.

## Positioning frame — where Maazah plays

A 2×2 to anchor against: **"flavor specificity"** (Y axis: from generic to culture-specific) × **"founder presence"** (X axis: from anonymous corporate to visible-named).

- **Generic + anonymous** → mass-market dips. Sabra. Boar's Head. The shelf incumbents Maazah is sitting next to but not competing as.
- **Culture-specific + anonymous** → small heritage brands that don't have a public founder face. They have the story but no one to deliver it.
- **Generic + visible-founder** → personality-led CPG brands without specific heritage (Trader Joe's-adjacent, the entrepreneur-as-mascot play). They have the face but not the depth.
- **Culture-specific + visible-founder** → **the quadrant Maazah owns.** Afghan-American heritage, mom's recipe lineage, two named-and-shown sisters who built it from a farmers market table.

The clearest one-line positioning: **"The first Afghan-American sauce + dip brand built for everyday tables — two sisters, one mom's recipe, eight SKUs designed to put on everything."**

The agency-replacement version (when comparing against in-house lift): **"What an in-house brand-team makes for you in three months, the engine makes weekly — in your voice, with the magic always in frame."**

## Tonal Territories — the 8 lanes Maazah naturally lives in

A cultural moment, viral recipe, or shelf event qualifies for the Pulse only if it lands in ≥1 of these:

1. **MAGIC-AS-MASTER-METAPHOR** — sauce-as-spell, drop-as-charm, "magic is real," the wordmark working overtime. *Test: does this make a literal product use feel like a small enchantment?*
2. **HERITAGE-WITH-SCALE** — Afghan flavors landing inside a Costco basket, a Sprouts aisle, a Midwest weeknight dinner. *Test: would this work as well in Minneapolis as in Brooklyn?*
3. **SAUCY-SQUAD HUMOR** — wordplay around *saucy / swicy / zestie / chut*, parenthetical asides, the brand's verbal identity. *Test: would this read as a @maazahmagic caption to someone who's never heard of the brand?*
4. **SISTERS-AT-THE-HELM** — the sibling-founder warmth, the back-and-forth, Yasameen + Sheilla together OR solo with each other clearly behind the camera. *Test: does this feel like a sister duo, not a brand statement?*
5. **MOM'S-MAGIC-GREEN** — the origin sauce, the farmers-market memory, the mom + aunties cooking. *Test: does this trace back to a specific kitchen?*
6. **PUT-IT-ON-EVERYTHING** — usage multiplication, the unexpected pairing, the "wait, on pizza?" reveal. *Test: would this make the viewer reach for their fridge?*
7. **CATEGORY-CREATION** — lentil-dip-as-new-shelf, sauce-as-character, sauce-shopping-is-not-the-condiment-aisle-anymore. *Test: does this teach without lecturing?*
8. **COSTCO-AISLE-MOMENT** — the retail-shelf hunt, the haul reveal, the "found it!" community shout. *Test: would a Costco-aisle fan account repost this?*

If a cultural moment doesn't map to any of these, it's not Maazah's pulse — even if it's viral. The engine's discipline is *what's trending that's tonally aligned with the brand*, not *what's trending*.

## Observed Voice Patterns (from @maazahmagic + the marketing site, calibrated 2026-05-25)

The voice is louder and more emoji-spiced than the average "founder-warm" CPG brand. ALL-CAPS is *not* rare — it's a structural posture for milestones and announcements. Lowercase warmth lives inside captions; the headline + the punchline often shout.

Observed across the screenshot set + site copy:
- Announcements: *"BIG🍃SAUCY🍃NEWS🍃Maazah just hit Sprouts NATIONWIIIIIDE!!!!"* — emoji as punctuation between caps words, exaggerated vowel stretching, exclamation chains.
- SKU intros: *"Meet the saucy squad:"* followed by a 🌶️/🥬/🥭/🍋 bulleted list, each SKU getting a one-line personality. *"Chili Aioli NEW!!! (she'll ruin your life... in the best way)"*
- Recipe captions: *"Maazah Red Pepper Lentil Dip turns crispy falafel, fresh tabouli, and pickled onions into a full-on situation 😅"* — mix of lowercase warmth + emoji + the trademark wordplay (*full-on situation*).
- Retail moments: *"Today we took her to @costco to see it on the shelves for the first time."* (Mom-coded warmth.) / *"Your lakeside hangs just got wayyy cooler 🏝️✨ Make a midwest snackle box stuffed with all your favorites..."*
- Wordplay: *"sweet + spicy = swicy"* / *"sunshine in a squeeze"* / *"KETCHUP AIN'T GOT CHUT ON THIS"* / *"We keep it spicy, not spammy"* / *"It's not sorcery. Or wizardry. It's just damn good flavor."*
- Music + grid posture: Roy Ayers Ubiquity "Everybody Loves The Sunshine," John Summit "Absolutely," original-audio collaborations. Pattern: groove-forward + summer-warm + dance-adjacent. Not chronically-online TikTok-meme audios; not generic wellness ambient.
- Emoji palette: ✨ 👀 💚 🥑 🌶️ 🥭 🍋 🥹 🍃 — soft + food-bright + a little wink. Not 💀😭🤡 (chronically-online); not 💪🔥💯 (gym-bro).
- Founder posts: more sister-as-narrator than first-person ("My sister and I built Maazah inspired by Mom's recipes..."). Family-coded. The sibling perspective is the voice.

## The Competitive Frame — May 2026 specifics

A market read as of the week of May 25, 2026. The dashboard's COMPETITORS array is live; this section names the strategic posture against each.

### The hummus aisle (the shelf neighbor we share, the voice we don't)

## Risks worth naming honestly

These come up in any partner conversation. Don't hide them.

1. **The Costco window is the most important commercial moment in front of the brand and it's narrower than it feels.** Costco's repeat-velocity math is reviewed on an 8-12 week cycle. If the brand spends those weeks on Expo-West recap content + general saucy posts and not on focused Costco-repeat content, the math wins by default and Maazah doesn't get the renewal it deserves. The engine's job is being the second + third sister who's already thinking about Week 6 in Week 1.
2. **"Lentil dip" category creation is a slow burn, not a quick win.** Shoppers don't know to search for it. Repeat-conversion happens through usage education, not flavor preference. The engine has to be patient — measure success on usage-content reach + recipe-share + saved-post velocity in the first 90 days, not direct ROAS.
3. **Sister bandwidth is the wedge AND the constraint.** Yasameen and Sheilla are doing TV (Stephanie's Dish), CPG operations (Costco demos, Sprouts coordination, Albertsons pitch prep), Expo West, family life, and being the face of the brand. The engine has to buy back time without becoming a *new* thing to manage. Weekly cadence + light-touch approval > more meetings + heavier process.
4. **The "magic" master metaphor needs careful seasoning — overused, it goes hollow.** Every brand has a power-word that turns into a cliche if it's in every line. The engine has to know when to lean in (milestones, hero moments) and when to let other language carry the weight (recipe descriptions, usage tips). Audit: no more than 60% of a week's brief mix uses "magic" in the headline or first line.
5. **The Afghan-American heritage story is sacred — first-gen voice can't be flattened for shareability.** The risk of the cultural-permission moment is that the brand starts performing the heritage instead of speaking from it. The engine's discipline: every heritage brief is structurally sister-fronted (it's their lived experience, not the brand's marketing), and the language is specific (Bolani, not "Afghan flatbread"; auntie's kitchen, not "traditional cooking").
6. **Founder-on-camera is the engine's biggest asset — under-using the sisters is the silent failure mode.** Both Yasameen and Sheilla are visibly comfortable on camera, the Booth-of-Year coverage put them in front of cameras at scale, and the Stephanie's Dish kitchen segment proved they translate to broadcast. The engine should default to 5-7 sister-fronted briefs per week (a mix of together + solo), with the rest as hands + product + recipe. Under-featuring them flattens the brand. The exact mix calibrates on the diagnostic.
7. **The retail-buyer voice and the consumer voice are different conversations.** The engine produces consumer content; the retail-buyer deck Maazah uses for Albertsons pitch + Costco extension review + chain-buyer meetings is a different artifact. Don't conflate. The OPERATE retainer can produce a retail-buyer narrative monthly if useful — separate output, separate register.
8. **The TikTok format gap is real and will take 4-6 weeks to close.** The platform-correct register (category-explainer + meme-fluent audio + period-spaced descriptors) is a different production discipline than IG. Expect 4-6 weeks of running the new pattern before TikTok engagement compounds to a healthy buyer-readable level. Set expectations honestly.
9. **Family appearances are off-limits unless and until the sisters say otherwise.** Mom Sajady, partner appearances, kid cameos — every public-image use of family is a per-instance choice the sisters make, not a standing permission. The engine NEVER drafts a brief that requires a family member to participate. Briefs use family as canonical origin reference (recipe attributed to Mom = fine; B-roll of Mom rolling dough = OPTIONAL upgrade only). This is a respect rule, not a workflow constraint.

## Anti-patterns — what the engine never does for Maazah

- **Call Maazah "hummus" or "hummus alternative."** It's lentil dip + sauce — its own category. Don't define by what it's not.
- **Lead with "Middle Eastern" when "Afghan-American" is the truth.** The specificity IS the brand. Generic "Middle Eastern" framing flattens what makes Maazah uncopiable.
- **Split the sister-duo into a single face.** Yasameen AND Sheilla are the brand. Brand voice should default to "we" / "my sister and I" / first-person plural.
- **Use "magic" in every headline.** It's a master metaphor, not a verbal tic. Seasoning, not the meal.
- **Frame the sauces as "exotic" or "adventurous."** They're everyday. The whole point is putting Afghan flavor on Tuesday-night chicken, not preserving it for a dinner party.
- **Punch at named competitors.** No "we're better than Sabra." Category critique only ("most dip aisles are just hummus on repeat"); names stay internal.
- **Treat "lentil dip" as an obvious category.** It's not, yet. Every lentil-dip brief should subtly teach the lentil-vs-chickpea / dip-vs-spread / Afghan-vs-Mediterranean lineage without lecturing.
- **Bury the Mom Sajady origin behind brand-voice marketing copy.** Mom is the source. Her presence, named or implied, is the depth that makes the milestones land.
- **Use generic "wellness" aesthetic.** Sunset light + grain-bowl-with-affirmation = death of Maazah's voice. The brand is groove-summer-warm, not yoga-mat-clean.
- **Recipe captions that don't loop back to the product use.** Every recipe brief lands the dish AND the dip. The recipe is the entry; the SKU is the exit.
- **Speak in supplement-brand register.** No "supports gut health." Say what the food does in food language.
- **Conflate any retail rollout with "we made it."** Each retailer is a step, not the destination. The engine keeps the founders thinking about the NEXT door, not basking in the current one. (But also, celebrate the wins loudly — both at once.)
