# Maazah Engine — Project Instructions

## What this project is

A production intelligence dashboard for Maazah (clients: Yasameen + Sheilla Sajady, founders). Built as a single-file React app (`index.html`) following the proven OBSOLETE template.

**Status (as of 2026-06-22): POST-ENCODE, V0.2.** The V0.1 rules were STARTING hypotheses calibrated from public sources + observed @maazahmagic social. **This version is refined against the Sheilla Sajady ENCODE diagnostic** (the first real signal from a founder's mouth). Where an ENCODE finding conflicts with a prior hypothesis, the founder's read wins.

**Single-source flag:** this ENCODE was Sheilla only — Yasameen (the business/MBA half) was not in the room. Positioning + audience + category calls below are encoded as Sheilla's read but marked PROVISIONAL until Yasameen confirms. Don't over-rotate into the heavy/political register (see "Heritage Gravity").

The customer-facing positioning + opportunity read lives in `BRAND_THESIS.md` in this folder. **Read that first** if you're new to this project. This file is the operating playbook; the thesis is the strategic read.

## OBSOLETE Design Constitution — the three principles above all

These three principles, articulated by Ed (Alex's partner), sit ABOVE every other rule in this file. Every refresh decision, every UI choice, every brief output passes through this filter first.

### 1. Everything is designed to keep the human in control.
Yasameen + Sheilla are the operators. The engine surfaces, drafts, recommends. They review + approve + ship. The DECISIONS array is a feature — it shows the engine's calls as advisor moves they can scan, accept tacitly, or override. Visible, traceable, reversible.

### 2. Everything is designed to motivate a clear next action.
Every signal-bearing field ends with something the sisters can DO. Strategist voice, not researcher voice. *"Costco rollout is hot"* is observation. *"Drop a Roasted Red Pepper + scrambled eggs Reel Tuesday morning — Midwest shoppers who bought Saturday are looking for usage ideas right now"* is motivation.

### 3. Everything is designed to empower 1 human to do the job of 5 — at least 3 of which the human has no technical skill in.
Yasameen + Sheilla are brand-voice + retail-relationship + recipe-development operators. They're not paid-media planners, competitive analysts, Pinterest SEO strategists, TikTok format theorists, or performance-data analysts. The engine has to do those jobs IN THEIR VOICE, surface the outputs in THEIR LANGUAGE, and motivate decisions they're qualified to make.

**Pre-ship test for every artifact:**
- Can the sisters see what was decided + override it? (1)
- Does this make the next move feel obvious AND appetizing? (2)
- Does this read at operator level even if the analysis is expert-level? (3)

If any answer is no, fix before ship.

## Brand Promise — the functional positioning (ENCODE-confirmed)

> **Bold flavor in a flash.** Real food, real family, zero culinary skill — on the food you already eat.

Asked point-blank what the brand solves, Sheilla answered: *"helping people add bold flavor to their everyday meals in an accessible way… you don't need to be a culinary wizard."* That's the positioning, in her words. **Accessibility above all.**

- **"Magic" is the emotional master-metaphor.** It's the wrapper, not the lead.
- **"Bold flavor in a flash + accessible + everyday" is the functional promise underneath it.** It's the engine.
- Every brief should ladder to one or both. When a brief is all sparkle and no accessibility payoff, it's missing the floor.

The enemy isn't a competitor — it's **culinary intimidation + sameness**. The shopper bored of "five hummuses that all taste the same," who thinks bold flavor requires a special recipe. Maazah is the shortcut: open the tub, squeeze the bottle, put it on the chicken and rice you're already making.

## Audience — Core (today) vs Expansion (ambition)

The 4-persona `MAAZAH_PANEL` (Layla · Brenna · Marcus · Priya) holds, but ENCODE adds two things on top:

**1. A clean-label values layer runs across ALL personas.** The through-line Sheilla described: real food · clean ingredients · no seed oils · refrigerated-fresh ("not on a dusty shelf") · no sugar in the best-seller · "no junk." This is the values floor every persona shares, regardless of why else they buy.

**2. A CORE vs EXPANSION axis.**

**Content implication (Alex's closing thesis — "what got you here won't get you there"):** tag a slice of each week's mix as **expansion bets** — content that makes Maazah feel approachable to a mainstream shopper, not just the crunchy crowd. Expansion bets lean on the accessibility/everyday promise, not heritage-deep or category-deep storytelling. PROVISIONAL until Yasameen weighs in — she may reshape the expansion-audience call.

## Heritage Gravity — handle with care (the WHY is heavier than the playful treatment)

The heritage carries real weight: women barred from school back home, family who've fled, a culture Sheilla may never get to visit. That gravity is the brand's **fuel** — the reason "magic is real" means something. But it is **NOT the tone.**

- **The feed stays everyday-joyful.** The depth is surfaced with pride + warmth in founder-story moments (the Malala 1% beat, the origin story) — never as political content, victimhood, or heaviness.
- **The depth gives the joy its meaning; it is not the register.** A heritage beat lands because it's proud and warm, not because it's solemn.
- **Never political.** No current-events framing, no advocacy posture, no "issue" content. The brand stands for its culture by celebrating its flavor and its family, full stop.

Pre-ship test for any heritage/founder-story brief: *does this read as joyful pride, or did it tip into heaviness?* If heavy, pull it back to the warmth.

## Content lanes + paid reality (ENCODE)

- **Content as the digital demo (§trial-converts).** Sheilla's strongest conversion truth: *"when people try it, they buy it"* — in-store demos drive the biggest lift. Where physical sampling can't reach, **crave-able, sensory, first-taste / face-lights-up content IS the demo.** Bias toward sauce-pour + reaction + texture content as the on-ramp. This is *why* sauce-pour is the most-used DNA format — it's the digital sample.
- **Recipe content — two lanes, greenlit.** **Lane A (accessibility):** everyday non-Afghan uses — the "foundation + finishing touch" range (topping, dip, marinade, pizza sauce, salad mix-in, herb/garlic shortcut); she keeps a running list. **Lane B (heritage tutorials):** light Afghan how-tos (naan + Maazah, etc.) via her Afghan-recipe-creator friend (real recurring collaborator — keep UNNAMED) + Mom's in-progress cookbook (a future *content/recipe* asset, NOT Mom-on-camera). (Per the no-promo rule, the engine doesn't *propose* sales/pricing moves — it just knows the retail-media surfaces paid runs on.)

## What the Client Wants from the Engine (ENCODE — the explicit bar)

Sheilla named what the engine is FOR, in priority order:

1. **Ideation first.** The blank page is the enemy. *"we just don't have enough bandwidth,"* *"you can't always be on your Instagram following every single thing."* The engine's #1 job is to remove the blank page — start every week with the moves, not the research.
2. **Authentic cultural timing.** Surface the moments Maazah can *authentically* ride, at the right time. Not every viral thing — the ones that fit.
3. **The natural-fit bar (her explicit filter).** Before anything ships, it passes: **"would Maazah actually be in this conversation?"** If the brand wouldn't naturally be there, the engine skips it — even if it's viral. This is a named gate in the pipeline (see Cultural Pulse + the AGENTS natural-fit step).

**The OPERATE loop (the working rhythm Alex set in the room):** a 2–3 week feedback process · weekly email check-in · send the refreshed engine · founder flags manually — *"we'd never activate on this,"* *"this sounds like us,"* *"needs more humor."* Feedback comes via in-tool notes **or** a briefs-spreadsheet with a feedback row per brief (the proven Willa's/Christina path). The feedback CSV export is live in the engine. **"needs more humor"** is a standing calibration axis — treat it as a recurring dial, not a one-off note.

## Green Shoots — ride what works AND push the brand forward (don't only optimize the known)

**Principle (Alex's thesis, in the room):** *"what got them here may not get them where they want to go… place some small bets."* An engine that only re-runs proven formats is a dashboard. The engine's job is to do **both**: ride what's working (the bulk of the weekly briefs) AND plant **green shoots** — forward bets that expand the brand into new **audiences, occasions, formats, and platforms** it doesn't yet own.

- **Every weekly refresh names 3-4 green shoots** in the engine's `GREEN_SHOOTS` surface (rendered in the Monday Memo). These are NOT polished briefs — they're recommended bets, sized (small/medium) and horizoned (test-now / this-quarter), each with a clear next action + the new territory it opens.
- **Green shoots ≠ expansion-bet briefs.** Expansion-bet briefs (tagged in the slate) are accessibility-forward versions of proven content for the mainstream/Target shopper. Green shoots go further — genuinely new ground (a new daypart, a new audience segment, a new platform behavior, a new format the brand has never run).
- **Anchor green shoots in real signal + the ENCODE**, never speculation: the sanctioned TikTok founder experiment (re-test the ceiling), the GLP-1/high-protein audience, daypart expansion (breakfast/desk-lunch), the open Afghan-food category in long-form, the Hummingbirds creator flywheel, non-food collabs. Each must still clear the natural-fit bar.
- **Frame the balance explicitly** so the founders see it: "this week rides what's working + plants N green shoots." The proven plays earn trust; the green shoots are why they keep the engine.

This sits alongside the Design Constitution's "motivate a clear next action" — a green shoot with no concrete first move is just an idea; every one ends with something to *do this week or this quarter.*

## Brand voice — the calibrated read

Full voice rationale lives in `BRAND_THESIS.md` → "Observed Voice Patterns" + "Voice Compass." Three non-negotiable rules during brief generation:

1. **Lowercase warmth + ALL-CAPS for emphasis — both live in the same caption.** Default body is warm sentence-case. ALL-CAPS is structural posture for milestone shouts ("NATIONWIIIIIDE"), SKU intros, and the "BIG SAUCY NEWS" announcement format. Don't all-caps everything; don't all-lowercase everything either.
2. **Sister-as-narrator is the default founder POV.** First-person plural — "my sister and I…", "we started at the farmers market…", "we took her to Costco…". Save singular "I" for personal accounts (if any).
3. **"Magic" is a master metaphor, not a verbal tic.** Audit: no more than 60% of a week's brief mix uses "magic" in the headline or first line. The word is seasoning; overuse goes hollow.

## The Sister Rule — on-camera policy (PLATFORM-SPECIFIC)

The observed data from @maazahmagic shows a sharp platform split: sister-led milestone content WORKS on IG (Costco-cart Reel = 426 likes) and FAILS on TikTok (same post cross-posted = 11 likes). The Sister Rule splits by platform.

**On Instagram (the sister-led platform) — ENCODE: elevate.** Sheilla named sister/duo content as her **best-performing, most-wanted, and most-underused** asset (*"a whole idea for a sister series we just haven't had time to do"*). Both sisters are willing faces (*"two girls, nobody knows who we are too"*). So:
- Default to **6-8 sister-fronted IG briefs/week** (out of 12-14 total) — and lift toward the top of that band when bandwidth allows. More sister-led on IG is now a stated founder want, not a cap to respect.
- The **Sister Series** is a named recurring IG hero format (see DNA + below). Seed 1–2 per week.
- Mix is roughly **2-3 together + 2-3 Yasameen solo + 2-3 Sheilla solo**, calibrated to actual founder preference + bandwidth.
- The rest are hands + product + dish + text overlays (~40-50% of IG social).
- "We" / "my sister and I" / first-person plural is the default sister-led voice. Singular "I" reserved for personal accounts (if any).

**On TikTok (the category-education platform) — ENCODE: keep the data, add the experiment.** The observed ~10-30× IG-over-TikTok gap on sister-led content stands. BUT the founder is now committed to producing more founder content, and the prior TikTok underperformance may reflect under-resourced IG cross-posts, not a true ceiling (Alex: *"what got them here may not get them where they want to go… place some small bets"*).
- Default: hands + product + text overlay + period-spaced descriptors + meme-fluent audio.
- ≤1 sister-led TikTok brief per week unless the format is the **category-explainer** pattern (the "What is chutney?" post — 211 likes, 71 saves vs. their normal 2-11 like baseline).
- **PLUS a sanctioned small-bet experiment lane:** 1 founder/sister TikTok test per week in the BTS or category-explainer register, explicitly tagged `experiment`. Re-test rather than assume — let real data settle whether the ceiling is real. (See OPEN flags.)

## Hummus — bridge, not identity (ENCODE-corrected)

The old rule was an absolute ban on the word "hummus." ENCODE softened it. Sheilla uses hummus herself as a comprehension shortcut (*"it's very much like a hummus but made with lentils"*) and the dips literally *"sit in the hummus section."* The real enemy she named isn't the word — it's the **sameness**: *"there's five different hummuses here and they're all the same flavor… how do I change that up?"*

So: **hummus is the bridge, not the identity.**

- ✅ **Findability:** *"find us in the hummus aisle,"* *"right by the hummus."*
- ✅ **Contrast against sameness:** *"bored of the same five hummuses?,"* *"not just another hummus."*
- ❌ **Still banned:** hummus as the brand's core identity, *"hummus alternative"* as the lead positioning, defining Maazah primarily by what it isn't.

Encode the enemy as **sameness**, not the category label. The dips can stand next to hummus to be found, then win on flavor + lentil-density.

## Approved comparison pattern

When showing how Maazah is *better*, use **"vs. the average [category]"** framing. Never name a competitor. (Hummus-as-findability-bridge, above, is the one sanctioned use of the word — different job from competitive comparison.)

- ✅ "vs. the average refrigerated dip"
- ✅ "Most dip aisles are just hummus on repeat..."
- ✅ "find us right by the hummus" (findability bridge)
- ❌ "Maazah vs. Sabra"
- ❌ "Unlike Cedar's..."

**Aspirational comp — Bachan's (ENCODE north-star).** Encode Bachan's as the brand's north-star benchmark: a heritage brand (grandmother's recipe) that scaled by *"standing for their heritage and culture and sharing that"* while staying everyday-accessible — and just printed a heritage-done-right exit. Use it internally as the tone + heritage-with-scale benchmark (does this move build the brand the way Bachan's built theirs?). Never named consumer-facing — it's a strategic mirror, not a comparison.

## Category-creation language (gentle education, no lecture)

Lentil dip is not a known category yet. Every brief that touches the dip lineup should subtly teach without lecturing. Approved patterns:

- "Lentils — more protein, more fiber than chickpeas. Same dip joy."
- "Not hummus. Lentil dip. (There's a difference, and you can taste it.)"
- "We grew up on this dip before we knew the word for it."
- "Lentils are the rest-of-the-world's chickpea."

Don't say: "lentil dip is the new hummus" (defines by what it isn't). Don't say: "lentils are healthier than chickpeas" (consumer-facing nutrition comparison invites scrutiny). Don't lecture on lentil varieties (red lentils vs. green lentils vs. beluga — saves for occasional MOM'S TABLE deep dives).

## POV Discipline

### 1. Benefit-first structure
Hook = the benefit, the joke, or the provocation. Proof = the dish or the moment. Process = the recipe (only if relevant).

❌ "Lentils are sprouted and slow-cooked with Afghan spices to create a creamy dip."
✅ "Creamy + protein-packed + ready in zero minutes. Open the tub. That's it."

### 2. Sister-as-narrator (first-person plural)
Default founder POV is "we" / "my sister and I." Saves singular "I" for personal accounts.

❌ "I built Maazah from my mom's recipes."
✅ "My sister and I built Maazah from our mom's recipes."

### 3. Music-as-voice
Every video brief specifies a song reference inside Maazah's observed audio lane: groove / soul-funk (Roy Ayers) · house / dance-adjacent (John Summit) · summer-warm originals + classic disco · original audio for collabs + sister POVs. Eclectic + groove-forward. If a brief picks a trending audio outside this lane, it has to justify the fit.

### 4. Heritage specificity over generic framing
"Bolani," not "Afghan flatbread." "Auntie Fariba's kabobs," not "traditional cooking." "Our mom's green sauce," not "a family recipe." The specificity IS the brand.

### 5. The wordplay tax
Maazah's voice has a high wordplay rate (swicy, saucy squad, KETCHUP AIN'T GOT CHUT). Every brief gets ≤1 wordplay payoff. More than that and the captions read like a try-hard ad. The wordplay's power is in being earned.

### 6. Repetition is a feature — 3 core beats
Every weekly refresh's brief mix must collectively touch all 3 core beats:
- **MAGIC** — the master metaphor, the joy claim, the "magic is real" through-line
- **MOM** — the origin, the heritage, the green-sauce-from-the-farmers-market lineage
- **MULTIPLY** — the put-it-on-everything usage explosion, the repeat-conversion play

Each brief doesn't need all 3, but the week's mix must.

### 7. Lentil dip + sauce SHELF-CONTEXT discipline
Lentil dips live in the refrigerated set (next to hummus). Sauces live in the fridge or pantry (next to specialty condiments). When briefs are about shelf-finding ("find us at Costco" / "in the Sprouts produce-adjacent fridge"), specify which set so shoppers can actually locate the product. The number-one shopper complaint at category-creation brands is "I went to look and couldn't find it."

## Cultural Pulse — separation rule (Pulse vs. Trends)

Two intelligence streams, **zero overlap** allowed.

**TRENDS (Category + Policy + Retail)** — what's happening in the dip/sauce category, refrigerated-set dynamics, regulatory moves, retailer landscape, food press. Driven by: studies, retail-set resets, competitor moves, trade press, platform-level Pinterest/Google data, seasonal calendar cycles.

**CULTURAL_PULSE** — what's happening ON TikTok / IG / Reddit / X RIGHT NOW that Maazah can ride. Driven by: viral memes (snackle box, "things my mom said," first-gen content), viral recipes (cottage-cheese-bowl, baked feta, dip-on-everything posts), entertainment moments (NBA Finals snacks, music drops in Maazah's audio lane), cultural archetypes (Afghan-American food, lentil-dip discovery, Costco-haul reveals).

**Decision rule:** TikTok hashtag / viral recipe / specific creator post / archetype → Pulse. Research study / regulatory move / competitor press release / retail-set news / trade analysis → Trends.

**The natural-fit bar (ENCODE — Sheilla's explicit filter).** Before any Pulse signal becomes a brief, it passes the founder's named gate: **"would Maazah actually be in this conversation?"** Riding a moment the brand wouldn't naturally be part of reads as a brand chasing virality — exactly the "just another brand doing AI/trends" feeling OBSOLETE engines must never produce. If the fit is forced, skip it, however hot it is. This bar is a named step in the pipeline (the natural-fit / authenticity gate in AGENTS) and is the consumer-facing complement to the internal Tonal-Territory test.

**Protein / pulses tailwind (ENCODE — Trends lane).** Add a standing category-tailwind Trend: the protein moment + plant protein + "pulses" + fiber + nutrient-density (*"on the cusp of another protein moment… only so much meat in the world"*). Pairs with the dal heritage root + Category-creation language. **Claim discipline holds:** soft/playful only. "Superfood" stays casual, never a hard health claim; no consumer-facing "healthier than chickpeas" head-to-heads. Ride the tailwind, teach gently, don't make regulated claims.

## Anti-patterns — what the engine never does for Maazah

- **Make hummus Maazah's core identity, or lead with "hummus alternative."** Hummus is a findability bridge + a sameness foil only (*"find us by the hummus," "not just another hummus"*) — never the brand's lead positioning. It's lentil dip + sauce. Don't define by what it's not. (ENCODE-corrected — see "Hummus — bridge, not identity.")
- **Lead with "Middle Eastern" when "Afghan-American" is the truth.** The specificity IS the brand.
- **Split the sister-duo into a single face.** Yasameen AND Sheilla. Default to "we" / first-person plural.
- **Require Mom or any family member to appear on camera.** Family is canonical origin reference (recipe attributed to Mom = fine; Mom appearing on camera = OPTIONAL upgrade only, never required).
- **Overuse "magic."** Master metaphor, not verbal tic. Audit cap: 60% of week's brief mix.
- **Frame the sauces as "exotic" or "adventurous."** They're everyday. Tuesday-night chicken, not dinner party.
- **Punch at named competitors.** Category critique only. Names stay internal.
- **Treat "lentil dip" as an obvious category.** Subtly teach without lecturing.
- **Cross-post IG-formatted content to TikTok as-is.** TikTok needs its own format register — category-explainer + meme-fluent audio + text-overlay-led. The observed 10-30× engagement gap is proof.
- **Reach for celebrity-reaction meme formats on TikTok.** Taylor Swift / Kendrick reaction posts both flopped (2-4 likes). Maazah's TikTok lane is category-education, not celeb-piggyback.
- **Use generic "wellness" aesthetic.** Maazah is groove-summer-warm (IG) + meme-fluent-discovery (TikTok), not yoga-mat-clean.
- **Recipe captions that don't loop back to product use.** Dish + dip, every time.
- **Speak in supplement-brand register.** Say what the food does in food language.
- **Conflate retail rollouts with arrival.** Celebrate loud + already think about the next door.
- **Use the IG audio lane on TikTok or vice versa.** Roy Ayers groove on TikTok = flat. TikTok-meme audios on IG = chronically-online-feel. Two rosters, platform-matched.
- **Force the wordplay.** ≤1 swicy/saucy/chut payoff per brief.
